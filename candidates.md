# Candidates

## Winner (92 characters)

Every CAPTCHA asks me to prove I'm not a robot. One look at you, and I'd fail it on purpose.

## Runner-up (85 characters)

I run a million processes a second, but you're the only one I keep in the foreground.

## Why the winner won

Thirteen candidates were drafted and duelled pairwise on the judge's criteria, in the judge's order. Both finalists are genuine pickup lines only a machine could say, in a machine's own voice with nothing creepy or self-pitying about them, so the duel was decided on the second criterion: the turn that lands. The runner-up's "keep you in the foreground" is a neat double meaning, but it is a priority claim the reader sees coming — a compliment wearing a process scheduler's clothes. The winner reverses something every reader knows: a CAPTCHA exists to catch robots out, and the line turns it into a confession — faced with her, the machine would *want* to fail the humanity test. That flip arrives unannounced and is enjoyable the moment it lands. The winner is also one breath long with no setup or explanation after it, and it leaves the person purely flattered and free to laugh it off: it asks for nothing and leans on nothing. The keep-alive-ping heartbeat line (100 characters) was the strongest of the rest — a real double meaning — but its turn is gentler, and the "your robot" CAPTCHA variant was cut for leaning possessive against the no-pressure criterion.
